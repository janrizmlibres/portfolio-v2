# PROTOTYPE — Warp or scroll (throwaway)

For [Warp or scroll](../../issues/17-warp-or-scroll.md). When the agent sends the visitor to a section, does the page scroll there, or does the bot warp the visitor through space to it? This is built on the page from *See the bot in the viewport*, with Janriz's picks there as defaults:
- surface B, the speech bubble plus a log (surface A, the chat panel, is left out);
- direct travel;
- lively wander;
- the first visit with the bubble over the bot.

It's rough now and tuned later: the warp's look and timing get tuned on the real site if it stays.

**Second round (after Janriz's first look).** The first warp felt thin, "an effect slapped onto the page", and he asked for a spiral (a tunnel, a warp drive) or a black hole the page gets sucked into. Two fuller styles now sit beside the first take, switched with the ✦ button in the bottom bar. In both, the page itself is the effect: what's on screen comes apart into its words, and the space is drawn behind the page.
- **Tunnel** (the default): the page streams past you in a spiral, down a tunnel with walls, towards a light at its end. The target section comes out of the depth and assembles.
- **Wormhole**: the bot slips under the page and opens a black hole in the middle. The page spirals into it, nearest words first, stretched towards the hole. The horizon swells over the screen, the scroll jumps, and the target section pours back out as the hole closes.
- **Streaks**: the first take, kept for comparison. The page zooms and fades under a layer of streaking stars.

**Third round (after Janriz's second look).** He liked the wormhole more, with four notes: the bot sometimes went behind the page's pieces and couldn't be seen; the wormhole's streaks felt "mid"; even at 2 s it felt short ("I want it to be an experience, as if the user is literally being transported"); and it needed an "oomph" at the start, with the bot acting rather than just turning. He also asked what happens if the visitor scrolls during it. So:
- **The wormhole is now a journey** of about 6 s (the ⚙ Journey slider, 3–10 s), and the default style. It has five acts, paced unevenly on purpose:
  1. **Launch:** the bot zips to the middle, turns its back and winds up, crouching and shivering, while the page draws back and a spark gathers behind it. Then it casts: a black hole bursts open, and a shockwave races out over the page, knocking the words aside and leaving open space behind it.
  2. **Swallow:** after a held beat, the whole page turns about the hole and falls in, nearest first, slowly and then all at once. The bot braces against the pull, antenna streaming towards the hole.
  3. **Plunge:** the bot looks back over its shoulder, grins, and dives in headfirst. The horizon swells over the screen.
  4. **Transit:** inside the wormhole, the throat opens out of the dark and we surge down it after the bot. It corkscrews ahead, does a barrel roll, and looks back and waves. The scroll jumps here, unseen.
  5. **Emerge:** a portal snaps open out of the light at the end, onto a white hole. The bot bursts out towards us, brakes and lands with a hop and a "ta-da", and the target pours out of the white hole into place as space gives way to the page.
- **No streaks.** The wormhole's space is one shader: stars and a faint nebula bent round the hole by gravitational lensing, a swirling accretion disk of gas, and the wormhole's throat of glowing gas. The tunnel and streaks styles keep their streaks.
- **The bot is always in front.** The space now has a canvas of its own behind the page, and the bot stays on its own small canvas over everything.
- **Scrolling during a warp** no longer fights it. The page is locked while a warp runs, and a fresh gesture hurries the warp to its end at 4× instead: a click or tap, Esc, a scroll key, or a wheel or swipe that starts after a pause. A trackpad's momentum from before the warp (C's own flick, say) isn't a fresh gesture, and momentum still running when it lands is swallowed before the page scrolls again.

**Fourth round (after Janriz's third look).** He wanted Pip to fly in real 3D space: moving back and forth in depth "as if it's actual 3D space", instead of sliding across the page "like a png". At the very start, a "swoosh" across the screen that circles round and then settles in the middle, instead of a swipe into place. Pip "can take as much screen space as he needs", has boosters "so he'd actually look like he's flying", and everything is fluid. The portal goes in front, because it sometimes couldn't be seen (behind the photo). And make it look cool: the visitor should go "wait woah what's happening.. wo- what.. woahhh. what's going onnn.. what the... holy.. what was that??". So:
- **Pip flies in 3D.** The visitor's eye is a camera in front of the screen, and the page lies at depth 0. Pip flies along smooth paths through points in that space: he grows as he comes at us and shrinks into the distance, and we see him from the angle his place on screen gives. In flight he leans into his motion like a drone, since his jets push along his body; he tips hard into turns and looks where he's going. When he slows down, he turns back to face us. At rest he looks exactly as before.
- **Boosters.** Two pods pop out of his back when the journey starts and fold away when he lands. With the thruster under his body, that makes three jets, each with a flickering flame, a glow at its nozzle, and a light on his underside. Every jet leaves a trail of fire: white-hot at the nozzle, cooling to orange as it spreads.
- **The journey is about 7.5 s** (the ⚙ Journey slider, 3–10 s), built as an escalation:
  1. *"wait…"* The boosters pop out and fire, the view jolts, and Pip rockets off. His wake shoves the photo and words aside.
  2. *"woah, what's happening"* He swooshes up over the page and right at us, big, across to the other side, and loops round the middle in a circle tilted back into the depth. The loop leaves a ring of fire. He brakes in front of the ring, his back to us.
  3. *"wo- what…"* The ring winds in on itself, faster and faster, into a spark, while Pip winds up and the view trembles. Then it bursts into a black hole in front of the page, with a flash and a shockwave that shakes the view and throws Pip back at us.
  4. *"woahhh"* Pip turns to face us and strains against the pull on full thrust, off to one side of the hole. His exhaust curls round into it. The page spirals in and the view tilts.
  5. *"what's going onnn…"* He glances back at the hole, grins at us, lets go, and dives in head first. The horizon swells over the screen as the view tips and rumbles.
  6. *"what the…"* Inside the wormhole, Pip drops back right past us, big, for a look and a grin. Then he does a barrel roll and boosts off towards the light, his exhaust streaming past us.
  7. *"holy…"* A portal snaps open, and a white hole flashes. Pip bursts out, swoops past us huge, and comes round.
  8. *"what was that??"* He brakes, lands with a hop, and folds his boosters away. The target has poured out of the white hole into place, and the card lights up.
- **The hole and the portal are in front of the page** now, on a new canvas between the page and Pip. The black hole punches through the photo instead of opening behind it, the pieces vanish at its horizon, and the throat, the portal and the white hole are never hidden. The starfield stays behind the page, so the words still fly in front of space.
- **A camera.** The page, both layers of space and Pip shake, roll and zoom together: a jolt at ignition, a tremble while charging, a hard shake at the blast, a tilt while the page is swallowed, and a rumble into the dive and down the throat.

**Fifth round (after Janriz's fourth look).** "Definitely better." He asked what Blender models could add, and took three of the four ideas: real boosters built into the body, a face that can react, and squash and stretch. He also wanted objects in the throat, if they're pieces of the page. And the opening's circling was "a bit too much": keep it to a minimum, one spiral fewer. So:
- **One circle, not two.** Pip's loop round the middle is now a quarter turn instead of 1¼, so the swoosh (up over the page, at us, across) and the loop make one circle round the screen. His trail of fire then closes itself into a ring round the middle, and the ring winds into the spark as before. The journey is about 7 s now (default 7 s, from 7.5 s). The old loop is still there to compare: ⚙ Pip's loop, ¼ to 1¼ turns (each extra turn adds its time).
- **The page in the throat.** Down the wormhole, 8–10 pieces of the section you just left tumble past at different depths: its biggest words, a card or two, the photo or a screenshot. They come out of the depth small and dim, lit by the throat and whiter as the light at its end grows, spin with it, and fade before they fill the screen. They keep off the side Pip flies past on, except one that shoots right past him as he barrel-rolls. On the light tier there are 6. ⚙ Throat: Page (default) / Empty.
- **Pip, remodelled in Blender** (`bot-flight.blend`, a copy of the sample bot; the one in *3D route verdict* is unchanged):
  - **Boosters built into his body.** Two hatches on his upper back, with hinge barrels and a panel gap round them, flip open. A pod slides out of the dark bay behind each and swings out to the side on a telescoping strut. Each pod has a nacelle, a graphite band, an accent stripe, three fins, and a nozzle bell with a throat that glows with thrust, as does the thruster under his body. Folding away runs it all backwards.
  - **A face that reacts.** New eyes that morph between five shapes: the usual capsules, round Os (wide), ^ ^ (happy), > < (squeezed) and spirals (dizzy). Wide when the blast throws him back and when he glances at the hole, > < at ignition, while winding up and while straining against the pull, ^ ^ for the grins, and on landing he's dizzy, spiral eyes spinning, then shakes it off and goes ^ ^.
  - **Squash and stretch** as shapes on the whole body: a crouch at ignition and in the wind-up, stretched long as he springs, as the blast hits, as he dives into the hole (tail thinning, head first) and as he bursts out of the white hole, squashed flat when he lands.

**Sixth round (after Janriz's fifth look).** The circling looked erratic: "I want it to be a clean circle, right now he does a lot of 'S' curves." Also: squiggly lines on Pip's back; at the end of a warp only the top element poured into place (warping to Projects, only "time" animated in); and a visitor may scroll while the chat is still deciding, so by the time the journey runs their scrolling sped it up to the end. So:
- **One clean circle.** Pip takes off up, spirals onto a circle round the middle, goes round it once, and curls in to stop in front of it, low and on his side of the middle, his back to us. The line never bends the other way: it is drawn as a circle on the screen, with spirals onto and off it whose radius eases slowly enough that they can't. Checked from eight parked spots on desktop and three on a phone, the old path bent back three or four times, the new one never. The depth only changes his size: far and small over the top, near and big at the bottom. His fire on the circle is the ring. As it closes, the arc now runs on round the circle instead of cutting across it. The ⚙ Pip's loop knob is gone.
- **Pip's back.** Three faults made the squiggles:
  - the left hatch opened the wrong way, into the body;
  - an open hatch was a single surface, so from below and behind it showed as a thin line;
  - the dark bays sat so close to the body that it poked through them in blotches, and their glossy visor finish mirrored the room's lights as more white blotches.

  Now both hatches flip up and out as solid flaps, and the bays are matte and lifted clear.
- **The whole target pours out.** When the scroll jumps, the camera has the page rolled and zoomed, and the target was measured through that transform, so most of it counted as off screen (11 pieces for Projects, some from the section left behind). It is measured as laid out now: 61 pieces for Projects, and every piece on screen pours out of the white hole. The pinned "Where I've worked" column had a fault of its own: when the scroll jumped, ScrollTrigger's pin still held it where the scroll had left it, so a jump from below skipped it and it then appeared in place. The pins are brought up to the jump before measuring (About now comes apart into 122 pieces, not 109).
- **Scrolling no longer hurries the journey.** A scroll during a warp is mostly not meant for it: someone still reading while the chat decides, a resting hand, a trackpad's momentum. Wheels, swipes and scroll keys are swallowed while it runs, and the page scrolls again once Pip lands. A deliberate click, tap or Esc still hurries it to the end, because 7 s with no way out is long on the third question in a row.

## Run

```sh
.scratch/redesign/prototypes/warp-or-scroll/serve.sh        # then open http://127.0.0.1:4175/
LAN=1 .scratch/redesign/prototypes/warp-or-scroll/serve.sh  # same, reachable from a phone on the network
```

The bottom bar switches the reading (← → keys work too):

| `?variant=` | Reading | A tool effect, nav link or in-reply link | Wheel, touch, keys |
|---|---|---|---|
| `A` | Scroll | ScrollSmoother scrolls there, the bot flies alongside, and the card lights up | native, smoothed by ScrollSmoother |
| `B` (default) | Warp for jumps only | the warp (nav links too, or not: setting) | native, smoothed by ScrollSmoother |
| `C` | Warp replaces scrolling | the warp | scroll inside the section being read; pushing past its edge warps to the next section |

In every reading, a target that is already close (under 0.6 of a screen away, or in C inside the current section) scrolls instead of warping. A warp for a small hop makes no sense.

The ⚙ button opens the settings, with a live readout of the last jump and its frame cost per phase.

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| Fire a tool effect | Fires `scroll_to` or `highlight_project` as the agent would, without asking |
| Style (`?style=`) | Wormhole (default), Tunnel or Streaks (also the ✦ button) |
| Pieces (`?grain=`) | What the page comes apart into: Words (default), Lines or Blocks. Anything with a border or a picture moves whole: a role, a work item, a screenshot, the photo. The light tier uses lines instead of words. |
| Journey (wormhole) | 3–10 s, the wormhole's whole journey (default 7 s); the light tier runs 10% shorter |
| Throat (`?throat=`) | Page (default): pieces of the page tumble past down the wormhole. Empty: the fourth round's throat. |
| Duration (tunnel, streaks) | 0.5–2 s, the scale of the tunnel's and the streaks' warp (default 1.0). The tunnel runs about 1.35 s from click to parked, streaks about 1.1 s. |
| Twist | 0–2; how far it spirals: the streaks' and walls' turns, the page's swirl, the bot's roll (default 1; 0 gives a straight tunnel). It turns the other way when the jump goes up the page. |
| Streak length | How long the stars stretch at full speed |
| Page blur (streaks) | The blur on the page as it leaves and arrives (default 6 px) |
| Star density | 300–6,000 stars (default 2,700) |
| Bot's lead | How long the bot sets off before the page does (default 0.15 s) |
| Scroll smoothing | ScrollSmoother's catch-up for reading scroll in A and B (default 1 s; 0 turns it off) |
| Destination known | When the tool effect fires after you send: **Canned** 0.8 s; **jump tool first** 1.7 s (measured); **wiki search first** 3.75 s (measured); **a router on every prompt** 0.25 s (the default since Janriz's pick: his config). The reply's first words arrive at 3.8 s, 5.5 s and 3.8 s respectively (measured), or straight after landing for Canned. |
| Nav links (B) | Warp / Scroll |
| Tier | Auto (light on phones and touch), Full, Light (fewer stars, no blur, a lower pixel ratio, 15% shorter) |
| Motion | Full / Reduced (the warp becomes a crossfade in B and C, and an instant jump in A) |
| Wander, View | as in *See the bot in the viewport*; 390 px puts the page in a phone-sized frame |

The sliders stay in the URL, so a link carries a tuning.

## How it's built

- `pieces.js` takes the page on screen apart for the tunnel and the wormhole:
  - It uses GSAP's SplitText for the words or lines. Anything with a border or a picture moves whole.
  - Each piece's path is a function of one progress value that a GSAP tween drives: out through the tunnel, in from the depth, into the hole, or out of it.
  - Every piece stays real DOM, moved by a transform and opacity. At the peak, the split is reverted, the scroll jumps, and the target is split.
- **Canvases.** The space is a WebGL canvas of its own (`space.js`), shown only while a warp runs. For the tunnel and the wormhole it sits **behind** the page (the page's background turns see-through), so the words fly in front of the space. For streaks it sits over the page, where its cover hides the jump. The wormhole adds a **front** canvas over the page and under the bot: see-through, and its light is *added* to what's under it (its alpha stays 0 where it only glows, which Chrome composites additively), so it glows over the page without hiding it. The bot stays on its own canvas over everything, so nothing hides it.
- `wormhole.js` is the wormhole's space: full-screen shaders with no particles, in two layers. GSAP tweens the numbers in its `state`, which mix the journey's views:
  - behind the page: open space, with stars and a faint nebula bent round the hole by a point-lens mapping (what's behind the hole shows as arcs round a ring), shown where the shock has passed;
  - in front of the page: a face-on accretion disk whose inner gas laps its outer gas, a photon ring and the horizon's shadow (which covers the page's pieces as they fall in);
  - also in front: the charge and the shock (a spark where the hole will open, then a ring of light racing out, which reveals space behind the page and refracts it);
  - also in front: the throat, mapped log-polar, so its walls keep their shape at every depth instead of smearing into streaks;
  - also in front: the exit portal, onto open space and a white hole, and a flash for the blows.

  While the throat covers the screen, the layer behind it isn't drawn.

  Its noise is a 64³ tiling 3D texture baked once at load, so each octave is one filtered fetch. Computing simplex noise per pixel cost 3–5 times as much. The shader runs at a capped pixel count (1.5 M pixels on the full tier, 0.6 M on the light tier, at most 1.5 px per CSS px). `bench.html` and `bench.js` time each view on its own.
- `stars.js` is the tunnel's and the streaks' space, drawn in two passes (its black hole and vortex mode belonged to the first wormhole, and are unused now):
  - **A full-screen shader** draws the tunnel's walls (spiral rails and rings in perspective, which move with the stars), the light at the vanishing point, and the black hole (an opaque horizon, a photon ring, and an accretion glow with spiral arms).
  - **The star field:** each streak is 6 segments, so it can curve along a helix (the tunnel) or an inward spiral round the hole (the wormhole, which runs outwards when `speed` goes negative).

  Each star's position comes from one accumulated `travel` value on the GPU. GSAP tweens `speed`, and the streak length, brightness and the star field's advance follow from it. GSAP also tweens `wall`, `core`, `hole`, `ring` and `disk` for the pass, and `cover`, the opaque page-colour backdrop that hides the jump in the streaks style.
- `flight.js` is Pip's 3D space. It holds:
  - the eye, a 45° camera in front of the middle of the screen, with the page at depth 0;
  - `path()`, cubic Hermite curves through timed keys, whose velocity is continuous (a key's tangent is the slope of the parabola through it and its neighbours, so uneven spacing doesn't overshoot);
  - `orbit()`, Pip's circle: drawn on the screen (a circle, with spirals onto and off it that can't bend the other way) and placed in the world at the depth each point should have, so depth changes his size and never the line;
  - camera shake noise.

  The journey's timeline doesn't tween Pip's position; his place is the path at the timeline's time, so hurrying or slowing the timeline carries him along.
- `trail.js` holds the boosters' trails: points in that 3D space, one ribbon per jet, drawn in the front canvas. They can slide round the circle Pip flew until they spread evenly round it (so an arc of fire runs on and closes into a ring), gathered into the ring's centre, pulled round into the hole, or streamed past us down the throat.
- `debris.js` is the page in the throat. When the journey starts, before the page comes apart, it measures what to take: the biggest words on screen (never twice the same), up to two cards (a role, a work item or a chip) and the biggest picture. From the ring beat on, it draws one a frame onto a canvas of its own and uploads it: words in the page's own font, colour and width (the page sets width with `font-variation-settings`, and a canvas takes the nearest named `fontStretch`), a card's slab, lines and words, the picture cover-fitted through its CSS filter. At the peak it plans each piece's pass once Pip's way out is known. In the transit it draws them in the front canvas, over the throat and under the trails, with a perspective camera matching `flight.js`'s. A piece's place is a function of one `travel` value that the journey tweens with an ease integrated from the throat's own speed, so hurrying or slowing the journey carries the pieces too. Fast pieces get two fading copies behind them, a cheap motion blur.
- `rig.js` is the bot's rig from *See the bot in the viewport* plus:
  - **A pose in 3D.** Its camera sits where the eye is, as seen from the bot. At rest it turns to face the eye with the old three-quarter view; in flight it leans into its velocity and acceleration and looks where it's going, blended by `pose.w` and eased so it never snaps.
  - **A canvas that follows the bot.** Its frame widens in flight so the flames aren't cut off, and its drawing buffer follows Pip's size on screen in steps: 1.5 px per CSS px while he flies, 1.25 once he's big, 1,280 px at most (768 on the light tier), so a close pass doesn't cost a full-viewport canvas.
  - **The boosters** are the model's own now. `boost.deploy` flips the hatches open (about their hinge, each its own way: the hinges are mirror images), then moves each pod out of its bay along a curve bent through a halfway pose (the model marks the stowed and halfway poses with empties), turning it as it goes and telescoping its strut; `back.out`'s overshoot lets it lock with a knock. Additive flame cones and nozzle glows hang off `Jet*` empties at the nozzles' exits, the shared `Glow` material brightens with thrust, and a jet light lights his underside.
  - **Shape keys** as weights on the emote layer: `eyeWide`, `eyeHappy`, `eyeSqueeze`, `eyeSpiral` (spun by `eyeSpin`), `stretch`, `squash`. The body's shape keys bake one deformation (`squash()` in `build-flight-bot.py`), and the rig moves the rigid parts by the same one (the pods, the main jet, the antenna's foot), so nothing floats off the body. The optimiser moves a mesh's origin to the middle of everything its shape keys reach, so each eye gets a pivot at its own middle to blink and spin about.
  - **An antenna on a spring** in flight.
  - **A `fly` layer** of flips, turns and rolls, and a rim light that the wormhole's glow drives.
  - **The acting:** `ignite`, `windup`, `cast`, `whoa`, `strain`, `glanceBack`, `grin`, `dive`, `soar`, `burst`, `brake` and `land`. They work through squash and stretch, lean, head, antenna and eyes, because the bot has no arms.

  main.js calls `rig.frame()` once per tick, after GSAP's update and after it has set the pose, so the canvas's place and what's drawn in it never disagree by a frame.
- `main.js` holds the jump:
  - `go()` picks scroll, warp, crossfade or instant.
  - `warp()` is one GSAP timeline over the bot, `stars.state` and the page. The page (ScrollSmoother's wrapper) is real DOM, which GSAP moves with scale, blur and opacity; nothing of the page is ever drawn to the canvas. `smoother.scrollTop()` jumps at the peak.
  - Section offsets come from layout (`offsetTop`), so neither the smoothing nor the warp's transform skews them.
  - C's input goes through GSAP's Observer, which prevents the default for wheel and touch.
  - While a warp runs, ScrollSmoother is paused and `guardInput()` swallows wheels, swipes and scroll keys. A click, a tap or Esc calls `hurry()`, which eases the warp's timeline to 4×.
  - The page is taken apart as laid out: `takeApart()` brings ScrollTrigger's pins up to the jump (`ScrollTrigger.update()`) and lifts the camera's transform off the page while it measures.
  - In the wormhole, `tick()` puts Pip on his path, moves the camera (`cam`: shake, roll, zoom, over the page, both canvases and the bot), places the bot's canvas where the eye sees him, and emits his exhaust. Between frames it adds points along the path, so a fast curve leaves a smooth trail.
  - Everything per frame runs on the jump's own time, so the slow-motion recordings and a hurried journey stay in step.
- `talk.js` holds the canned replies, plus `jumpFor()`, the tool effect each question fires. "Is he available?" jumps to Contact here, although the real chat called no tool for it.
- The backdrop's sticky "Where I've worked" heading is pinned with ScrollTrigger, because `position: sticky` can't work inside ScrollSmoother's moved content.

## Checks

- `check.js` is a headless smoke run (GPU on) that screenshots into `shots/`: `node check.js [A B C chat rm mobile journey guard C-journey]`.
  - `journey` films the wormhole at a quarter speed, there and back, and checks that nothing is left behind (`TIMES=` picks the moments, in ms of the journey).
  - `guard` scrolls, clicks and presses keys during a warp. A wheel stream running on into a warp, and a fresh wheel at 1.3 s, neither hurry it nor move the page (7.15 s in all), and it lands on the target. A click at 2 s hurries it (3.42 s), and so does Esc (3.12 s), while PageDown before it does nothing. After landing, the wheel scrolls the page again.
  - `C-journey`: in C, the flick that sets off the journey doesn't hurry it.
- `measure.js` measures frame cost with no screenshots while measuring, and writes `shots/frame-cost.json`. Headless Chrome, ANGLE on Metal, Apple M5, deviceScaleFactor 2, top → Athena → top three times per config:

  | Config (sixth round, the wormhole configs) | Frames per jump phase | Worst frame | WebGL per frame, all canvases (parked → warping) |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | **B wormhole, words, full tier, 1440** | 59–60 fps | 26 ms (1 frame) | 0.68 → 3.8–5.4 ms |
  | **B wormhole, full tier, 1920×1080** | 59–60 fps | 25 ms (1 frame) | 0.59 → 3.5–5.4 ms |
  | **B wormhole, light tier (lines)** | 59–60 fps | 24 ms (1 frame) | 0.55 → 1.8–2.3 ms |

## Known rough edges (for the build, not this call)

- **Spinning is harder on the stomach than zooming.** Reduced motion gets the crossfade in every style, but many people who get motion sick never set it. The twist is a slider for that reason.
- **The journey is about 7 s,** and it plays on every jump. A click, tap or Esc hurries it, but a visitor who asks three questions in a row sits through three journeys. The build could shorten it after the first, or use the tunnel for later jumps.
- **Only the wormhole flies in 3D.** The tunnel and streaks keep the third round's flat bot, with no boosters. Regular moves (wandering, shooing, dragging) don't use the boosters either.
- **The model weighs more.** `bot-flight.glb` is 221 KB, 98 KB gzipped, against 36 KB for the sample bot. Most of it is the squash and stretch keys, which cover every mesh of the body. For the build: keys only on the meshes that show the shape, or the squash and stretch done in a vertex shader instead.
- **The hatches are on his back,** so from the front you see the pods come out at his sides but not the hatches opening. You see them from behind as he hovers in front of the ring.
- **Where Pip stops in front of the ring moves** with where he took off: the circle ends as near a whole turn as lets it stop in the bottom third of the circle, on his side of the middle. From the top of the screen the circle is shorter (two thirds of a turn); the ring still closes.
- **The throat's words are redrawn, not the page itself.** A canvas takes the nearest named font width (the name's tight Archivo is drawn "extra-condensed"), and a piece seen from behind shows its text mirrored.
- **Pip's final look is still open** (*The mascot's look*). What carries over to any mascot is the approach: named hatches and nozzle points, eyes that morph, squash and stretch on the whole body.
- **Additive light washes out over white.** The trails, the disk and the flash add light, so over the white photo they go white rather than orange.
- **The view rolls and zooms** while the page is swallowed and in the dive (to about 1.25× at the most, so the corners stay covered). On a phone that zoom crops the edges noticeably.
- **Big pieces fly past big.** In the tunnel, the photo and the name grow several times over as they pass. That is dramatic but a lot of grey.
- **The highlight's own CSS transition** shows the card's text dark for a frame before its fill arrives. This predates the warp and happens in every style.
- The log (surface B's "Log" button) had lost its styles in the first round; they're back.

- **Where the bubble opens after landing.** For a card, it opens under the card. For a section, the bot parks after the heading and the bubble opens beside it. On phones it still covers part of a tall card. Long replies push it up over what it talks about.
- **Input during a warp** isn't queued. Scrolling is dropped (the page stays where the jump lands), and a click, tap or Esc hurries it.
- **Big pieces are stretched hard** as they near the hole (the name, the photo) and can fly off the top of the screen for a moment on their way in.
- **The white hole's glow** is now in front of the page, so it lies over the landing text for a moment while it fades (it fades sooner than it did).
- **C's hero** has only a few px of reading before a push warps. C cuts the page into scenes the size of its sections.
